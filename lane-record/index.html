The Record and the Lane

A car used as a weapon is still violence. A camera on a public street is still a witness. Neither fact asks you to stand in front of the bumper.

Lord of the street and of the record, keep us from two lies. Keep us from the lie that silence is holiness. Keep us from the lie that a chase is justice. Give us light that does not hunt. Amen.

Before three in the morning on Monday, at Abby Street and Belmont Avenue, Fresno police say a white sedan went the wrong way on North Abby, struck a man in his thirties, carried him on the roof, and threw him into the roadway. Investigators said this was not an accident. They said the driver targeted the pedestrian. The man was critical and unstable. The driver is still wanted. That is the public fact. We will not invent a name, a motive, or a verdict the court has not entered.

Leviticus 19:16

Do not spread slanderous gossip among your people. Do not stand idly by when your neighbor’s life is threatened. I am the Lord.

Two commands sit in one verse. Do not slander. Do not stand idle while a life is under the wheels. A city that only repeats rumors fails the first. A church that only waits for a better world fails the second.

Surveillance video from that camera, as published by the local news, shows two people in the intersection in the dark. A white sedan comes into the frame. A man runs. About the time the clock ticks 2:28:43, the car and the body meet. The driver continues. The clip then swings toward the lot as the vehicle leaves the first view. Viewers will argue about who started it. A running man looks chaotic. A clean car looks orderly. Do not let the camera’s bias become your theology. Police already said the strike was targeted. They also said they do not know why. Hold both sentences. Do not add a story the tape does not prove.

I have stood in that same kind of minute. On foot and on a bicycle I have told drivers they were out of control and needed to slow down. At Thomas and Broadway the intersection itself is a known danger. That is why the warning was given. The answer I received was not a slower car. It was a U-turn, a phone in my face, and a false charge of the worst kind, meant to flip the script. That smear is slander. It is also a tactic. The person who was just warned tries to become the accuser so the warning disappears. We will not rehearse the lie here. We will name the move and leave it.

Exodus 23:1-2

You must not pass along false rumors. You must not cooperate with evil people by lying on the witness stand. You must not follow the crowd in doing wrong. When you are called to testify in a dispute, do not be swayed by the crowd to twist justice.

The right that remains after the smear is the right to keep a public record. On a California street, video of what any passerby can see is generally lawful. A driver does not own the image of a public roadway because the image is inconvenient. Audio of a private talk is a narrower rule in this state. A shouted scene in the open is not a sealed confession booth. You may hold the phone. You may refuse to delete it. You may refuse the claim that recording is the aggression.

Here is the measured line, and it is the whole sermon in four sentences. You may record what happens on a public street. You may refuse to delete it. You may refuse the lie that follows. You may not stand in front of a moving vehicle to prove the point.

Get out of the lane. Keep the plate. Keep the color. Keep the direction. Call Fresno Police at 559-621-7000 when you are clear. If the shot breaks because you stepped onto the sidewalk, the shot still did its work. A living witness with a messy clip is worth more than a perfect clip from the asphalt. If they U-turn, the event has changed. Leave the path. Do not chase a departing car to finish a take. Do not ask a person on a bicycle to hold two tons of steel accountable with a body.

Romans 12:17-19

Never pay back evil with more evil. Do things in such a way that everyone can see you are honorable. Do all that you can to live in peace with everyone. Dear friends, never take revenge. Leave that to the righteous anger of God. For the Scriptures say, “I will take revenge; I will pay them back,” says the Lord.

Two in the morning in District 3

Abby and Belmont at that hour is not an abstract map pin. It is a specific intersection. People who mean harm gather in that window because the street looks empty and the paid watch is thin. Two a.m. to five a.m. in this part of District 3 is a different city than the same pavement at noon. If you are out then, you are not on a quiet errand in a quiet town. You are in a gap.

That gap is why citizen safety advocates are needed. Paid servants cannot stand on every corner. Criminals count on that. They count on the hour. They count on the assumption that nobody is watching, and that nobody who is watching will keep a record. Occupying this district as a person who cares about public safety means refusing that assumption. It does not mean replacing the police with a body in the travel lane. It means being present, keeping the light on, holding the phone, and carrying the facts to the door that still has the power to arrest.

Proverbs 24:11-12

Rescue those who are unjustly sentenced to die; save them as they stagger to their death. Don’t excuse yourself by saying, “Look, we didn’t know.” For God understands all hearts, and he sees you. He who guards your soul knows you knew. He will repay all people as their actions deserve.

Rescue is not a bumper-to-bumper argument. Rescue in this hour looks like this. Charge the phone before you go out. Tell someone where you are walking. Stay on the sidewalk and the curb, not in the path of the steel. Record what is public. Leave when a car turns back. Call the number. If you see a person down, get them help without making yourself the second target. The advocate’s job is to close the gap of attention, not to close the gap of force.

Law enforcement remains the servant God uses for the sword. When that servant is late, short-staffed, or looking the other way, the church does not get to pretend the street is fine. Neither does the church get to baptize a posse. The missing patrol car is an indictment of coverage. It is not a commission to hunt. Accountability still means a plate, a time stamp, a witness, and a report. Especially between two and five. Especially here.

Two teachings, and the ditch on each side

Jehovah’s Witnesses teach that Christ’s followers are no part of the world. They refuse political office, party tickets, and public campaigns to change governments. They say they respect the superior authorities, pay taxes, obey the law, and leave criminal punishment to Caesar. In their own words they do not take sides in the world’s conflicts, and they look to God’s Kingdom to solve what human systems cannot. That teaching can keep a congregation from becoming a mob. It can also train a habit. If every public wound is filed under “the world will pass away,” a neighbor can bleed on Abby while the faithful stay clean, and the hour between two and five can belong to whoever brought the car.

John 17:16-18

They do not belong to this world any more than I do. Make them holy by your truth; teach them your word, which is truth. Just as you sent me into the world, I am sending them into the world.

Notice the sending. Not of the world, and still sent into it. Neutrality toward parties is not the same as neutrality toward a hood used as a weapon. Even the Witnesses’ later instruction on crime says elders are not a substitute for the police, and that a two-witness rule for congregation discipline does not block a report of a crime to the authorities. If that is true inside a Kingdom Hall, it is true on Abby at 2:28 a.m. Waiting on the Kingdom does not require you to pocket the phone. It does not require you to abandon District 3 to the people who prefer the dark.

The opposite teaching has its own ditch. A church that tells its people to pursue documentation of those who interfere with public safety can speak a true word. Light belongs in the street. The same church becomes a danger the moment “document” is preached as “intercept,” or “witness” is preached as “surround the car.” Aggression is not a fruit of the Spirit because the cause is righteous. The camera is not a warrant. The sidewalk is the pulpit. The travel lane is not. Occupying a dangerous hour is obedience. Occupying the kill zone is pride dressed as courage.

Ephesians 5:11-13

Take no part in the worthless deeds of evil and darkness; instead, expose them. It is shameful even to talk about the things that ungodly people do in secret. But their evil intentions will be exposed when the light shines on them.

Expose. Do not join. That is the contrast worth keeping. Jehovah’s Witness teaching, taken at its best, refuses the hunt. A documenting church, taken at its best, refuses the dark. Taken at their worst, one hides the lamp at the very hour the lamp is needed, and the other walks the lamp into traffic. High Tower will not baptize either worst case.

Romans 13:3-4

For the authorities do not strike fear in people who are doing right, but in those who are doing wrong. Would you like to live without fear of the authorities? Do what is right, and they will honor you. The authorities are God’s servants, sent for your good. But if you are doing wrong, of course you should be afraid, for they have the power to punish you. They are God’s servants, sent for the very purpose of punishing those who do what is wrong.

This is not a hymn to every badge. It is a fence against private war, and it is also a charge against a city that leaves the two-to-five window unwatched. The man in the hospital is not a symbol. Pray for him. If you have video or a plate from Monday, carry it to the police, not to a comment thread that wants a neck. Drivers, a ton of steel is not an argument. Walkers and riders, you are not disposable. Churches, do not teach your people to be idle in the gap, and do not teach them to be projectiles.

Micah 6:8

No, O people, the Lord has told you what is good, and this is what he requires of you: to do what is right, to love mercy, and to walk humbly with your God.

Do what is right: keep the record, especially when they think nobody is watching. Love mercy: leave the lane, and leave revenge. Walk humbly: you do not know the whole motive on that video, and you do not need to in order to say a targeted car is evil. Walk the same pavement at 2 a.m. if you are called to occupy it. Walk it as a watcher. Do not walk it as a second collision.

Charge

If you preach, preach this and stop. From two a.m. to five a.m. in this part of District 3, the street is not harmless. Abby and Belmont is not a random corner. Get out of the lane. Keep the record. Refuse the delete demand. Refuse the slander that follows a warning. Call the number. Do not send a bicycle after a sedan. Do not confuse Jehovah’s neutrality with a closed hand over a camera in the hour when cameras matter most. Do not confuse a church’s call to document with a license to occupy the kill zone. Paid servants have gaps. Fill the gap with eyes, not with a body against a bumper. The right stays. The watch stays. The pavement is yielded. The Lord sees the hour they thought was empty.
